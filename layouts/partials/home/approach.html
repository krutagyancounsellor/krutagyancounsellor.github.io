A person-centred foundation, informed by different perspectives.

My approach to counselling is grounded in person-centred principles: respecting each person's experience, recognising their autonomy and allowing their concerns and priorities to guide the process.

I also draw on my understanding of different psychological perspectives to explore experiences from more than one angle. Our thoughts, emotional responses, relationships and beliefs do not exist in isolation. They are shaped by personal histories, family environments, cultural influences and the expectations we encounter.

My academic learning and professional development have introduced me to perspectives from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), Rational Emotive Behaviour Therapy (REBT), Gestalt approaches and Transactional Analysis. These inform how I think about patterns of thought, emotional responses, present experiences, beliefs and ways of relating to others.

Rather than applying a particular framework as a fixed method, I bring these perspectives into my understanding of the concerns you share. The focus remains on your experience and on exploring what feels relevant to you.

This approach leaves room to question familiar assumptions, consider alternative interpretations and examine how the different parts of your life intersect.

The aim is not to fit your experience into a theory, but to understand your experience in its own context.
