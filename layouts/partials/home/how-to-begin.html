You may be navigating a life transition, reconsidering familiar roles, questioning beliefs you have held for years, or trying to understand why certain experiences affect you in ways you did not expect. Perhaps you know what feels difficult, but are unsure how to make sense of it. Or perhaps you simply feel that something in your life or your understanding of yourself has changed.

You do not need to arrive with a clear explanation or a specific goal. Counselling can begin with what feels important to you right now.

We can also consider whether the concerns you bring and the support you are looking for are a suitable fit for my practice.

The initial consultation is an introductory conversation, not a counselling session. It is not intended for in depth therapeutic exploration or treatment, and there is no obligation to continue afterwards.

{{- /* Step 02 */ -}}

02

Decide whether you would like to continue

After the initial consultation, you can take time to decide whether you would like to proceed. If you choose to continue, we can discuss the practical arrangements and schedule your counselling sessions.

You may bring a specific concern, a change in your circumstances, a recurring difficulty or questions that are still taking shape. You do not need to know exactly what you want to work through before beginning.

{{- /* Step 03 */ -}}

03

Explore your experiences in the context of your life

Counselling offers a space to reflect on how you understand yourself and what you are experiencing. This may involve looking at the roles you occupy, your relationships, family and cultural expectations, personal beliefs and the changes you are navigating.

My approach has a person centred foundation and is informed by different psychological perspectives. Our conversations are guided by your experiences and concerns, rather than by a predetermined explanation of what you should be feeling or doing.

Together, we can examine familiar patterns, question assumptions, consider alternative perspectives and explore how different aspects of your life influence one another. The aim is not to provide ready made answers, but to support a more considered understanding of your experiences and what they mean to you.
